# Developing Your Elevator Pitch: Checklist

> **The goal:** write and deliver a 30–60 second (75–150 word) answer to "Tell me about yourself," as if you're interviewing for a position in your chosen industry.

## Step 1: Brainstorm

Take notes on each topic below. You don't have to answer every question. Just jot down whatever comes to mind.

- [ ] **Your background:** Where do you go to school, and when do you graduate? Have you had any jobs, internships, or volunteer roles? What experiences have shaped you the most?
- [ ] **Your skills and strengths:** What are you especially good at? What sets you apart from others?
- [ ] **Your accomplishments:** What project or achievement are you most proud of? How did you overcome a challenge or help a team succeed?
- [ ] **Your motivation:** What drives you? What parts of your future career are you most excited about?
- [ ] **What makes you unique:** What interests, experiences, or perspectives shape who you are?
- [ ] Circle or highlight the **2–3 ideas** from your notes that you're most proud of. These will be the heart of your pitch.

## Step 2: Write Your Pitch

Write one or two sentences for each part below. If you get stuck, open the sentence starters under each part. Fill in the blanks (___) with your own details, then change the wording so it sounds like **you**.

### Part 1: Introduce Yourself

- [ ] Write your introduction.

<details>
<summary>Need help getting started? See sentence starters.</summary>

- "My name is ___, and I am a ___ at ___."
- "Hi, I'm ___. I'm a ___ graduating in ___."
- "My name is ___, and I'm a student at ___ who is passionate about ___."

</details>

### Part 2: Share Your Background and Skills

- [ ] Write about your background and your key skills.

<details>
<summary>Need help getting started? See sentence starters.</summary>

- "Outside of my regular classes, I ___."
- "I have experience with ___, including ___."
- "I'm especially skilled at ___, which I've used to ___."
- "One of my strengths is ___. For example, ___."

</details>

### Part 3: Highlight an Accomplishment

- [ ] Write about one specific accomplishment. Include what **you** did and what the result was.

<details>
<summary>Need help getting started? See sentence starters.</summary>

- "One project I'm proud of is ___, where I ___."
- "Recently, I ___, which taught me ___."
- "When I faced ___, I ___, and as a result ___."
- "I helped my team ___ by ___."

</details>

### Part 4: Show What Makes You Unique

- [ ] Write about what makes you unique.

<details>
<summary>Need help getting started? See sentence starters.</summary>

- "Beyond ___, I love ___."
- "Something that makes me different is ___."
- "My experience with ___ has given me ___."
- "In my free time, I ___, which has taught me ___."

</details>


### Part 5: Close with Your Goals

- [ ] Write a closing sentence about your goals or what you're looking for.

<details>
<summary>Need help getting started? See sentence starters.</summary>

- "I am excited to pursue ___ because ___."
- "I'm looking for an opportunity to ___."
- "In the future, I hope to ___."
- "I'm a ___ and ___ student, and I can't wait to ___."

</details>

Here's an example pitch:

> My name is Gwyneth Ormes, and I am a high school senior graduating in 2018. Outside of my regular classes, I have a strong passion for computer science and leadership. I currently compete in CyberPatriot, an international cybersecurity competition, and I recently earned my Girl Scout Gold Award by creating a camp that introduces fifth-grade girls in my community to computer science. Beyond tech, I love staying active and creative. I work as a camp counselor for a high ropes course, and I serve as the costume crew head for my school's theatre program. I am a dedicated and driven student and I am excited to pursue computer science in college, and eventually as my career.

Notice how it follows the five parts: an introduction, background and skills, specific accomplishments, what makes the speaker unique, and a closing goal.

## Step 3: Put It Together

- [ ] Combine your five parts into one paragraph.
- [ ] Add transition words so it flows smoothly, like "Outside of class," "Recently," "Beyond that," or "In the future."
- [ ] Count your words. Your pitch should be **75–150 words**.
- [ ] Read it out loud and time yourself. It should take **30–60 seconds**.
- [ ] Too long? Cut anything that doesn't support your 2–3 main ideas. Too short? Add a specific detail or example.
- [ ] Check that it sounds professional, the way you'd talk in a real interview.

## Step 4: Practice with a Peer

- [ ] Deliver your pitch to a classmate.
- [ ] Ask them for feedback:
  - [ ] What was the most memorable part?
  - [ ] Was anything confusing or unnecessary?
  - [ ] Did I sound confident and natural?
- [ ] Revise your pitch based on their feedback.
- [ ] Practice until you can say it smoothly **without reading it**.

## Step 5: Deliver Your Pitch

- [ ] Choose how you'll deliver it: record it as audio or video, or present it in class.
- [ ] Deliver your pitch! Make eye contact (or look at the camera), speak clearly, and smile.
- [ ] ✋ Submit your recording or give your pitch to your instructor.
