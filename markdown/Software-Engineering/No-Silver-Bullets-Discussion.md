# "No Silver Bullets" Discussion

## Your Task

Read the article [No Silver Bullets: Essence and Accidents in Software Engineering](https://www.cgl.ucsf.edu/Outreach/pc204/NoSilverBullet.html) and be prepared to discuss the following questions in class. 

1. Brooks distinguishes between the "essence" and "accidents" of software engineering. In his own terms, what makes up the essence, and what are some examples he gives of accidental difficulties?
2. Brooks argues that no single development in software engineering will produce a tenfold improvement in productivity within a decade. What reasoning does he give for this claim?
3. Brooks lists four properties inherent to the essence of software: complexity, conformity, changeability, and invisibility. Pick one and explain, in your own words, why Brooks considers it an essential (rather than accidental) difficulty.
4. Brooks was skeptical that AI (as it existed circa 1986) could meaningfully attack the essence of software complexity. Given how AI has evolved, do you think Brooks's skepticism still holds, or has something fundamentally changed?
5. Modern AI coding tools reduce a lot of accidental difficulty (boilerplate, syntax lookup, translating requirements into code). Do you think they also touch the essential difficulty? Or are they just moving the bottleneck?
6. If a true "silver bullet" for software engineering did emerge, what would have to be different about how we build software? Try to imagine what such a bullet might look like, and what new "essence" or difficulty might emerge to take its place.
