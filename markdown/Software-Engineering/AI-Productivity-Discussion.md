# AI Productivity Discussion

## Objective
1. Analyze multiple sources to develop a argument
2. Research the impact of AI tools on modern software development




## Your Task

You will analyze multiple sources to develop an arugement to answer the question: do AI coding assistants actually make developers more productive?


Here's a source list that spans the full range of findings — vendor-funded studies showing large gains, independent research complicating the picture, and syntheses that show why the numbers disagree. I've grouped them so students can see *where* the disagreement comes from (who funded the study, what was measured, lab vs. real-world).

**Studies showing strong productivity gains (mostly vendor-affiliated or vendor-adjacent)**
1. Peng et al., "The Impact of AI on Developer Productivity: Evidence from GitHub Copilot" (Microsoft Research / arXiv, 2023) — the original controlled experiment; treatment group finished a coding task 55.8% faster.
2. GitHub's own blog post on the Peng et al. study and related survey data on developer satisfaction.
3. Cui, Demirer et al., "The Productivity Effects of Generative AI: Evidence from a Field Experiment with GitHub Copilot" (MIT, 2024) — real-world RCT at Microsoft and Accenture measuring pull-request output rather than a lab task.

**Independent or contradictory findings**
4. METR's 2025 randomized controlled trial finding experienced open-source developers were about 19% *slower* with AI tools, despite believing they were faster — the single most cited counter-study.
5. GitClear's analysis of 211 million changed lines of code (2020–2024) showing rising code churn, used as evidence for quality/maintainability trade-offs rather than pure speed.

**Recent syntheses that weigh both sides (good for showing students how to reconcile conflicting evidence)**
6. Farrag, "The Productivity-Reliability Paradox" (arXiv, 2026) — explicitly reconciles the 20–56% task-level gains against the 19% RCT slowdown and flat organizational metrics.
7. "The Impact of AI Coding Assistants on Software Engineering: A Longitudinal Study" (arXiv, 2026) — mixed-methods survey of professional developers on perceived vs. actual productivity.
8. Panto AI, "AI Coding Productivity Statistics 2026" — industry synthesis arguing gains are real at the task level but don't reliably translate to organizational outcomes without process changes.

**Data on adoption and skill effects**
9. Anthropic, "How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills" (Jan 2026) — observational study of Claude.ai usage data, raising the question of whether speed gains come at the cost of skill development.
10. Stack Overflow's Developer Survey (annual) — large-scale self-reported data on developer sentiment toward AI tools, useful as a "perception" source to set against the harder productivity numbers.

A good framing question for students: which of these studies measure *speed on an isolated task* vs. *real organizational output* — and does the answer to "are developers more productive?" change depending on which one you pick?
